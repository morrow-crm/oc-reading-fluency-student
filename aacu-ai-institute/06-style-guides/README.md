# Style Guides

The two references used to revise the Action Plan draft. Both were supplied by the team.

| File | Author / source | What it governs |
|---|---|---|
| [justin-admin-docs.md](justin-admin-docs.md) | Justin Lewis (English faculty, on our Institute team) | House style for faculty-facing institutional documents at Olympic College |
| [no-ai-trace.md](no-ai-trace.md) | Built on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" (WP:AIWTW) | Diagnostic and rewrite protocol for removing AI-writing markers |

## What each one contributed

**justin-admin-docs.md** is an Olympic College document. It carries institutional vocabulary
(ctcLink, Canvas, division office, English Coordinator, CiHS, Running Start, PSNS substitution
rate, Accuplacer), CBA awareness, and a register calibrated by document type. Rules that drove
the revision:

- **No em dashes.** Stated as an absolute, not a preference. Replace with commas, semicolons,
  parentheses, colons, or a restructured sentence.
- Three-part lists used as default organizational structure are listed under "Patterns That
  Signal AI-Generated Text."
- Also flagged there: "It is important to note" constructions, uniform paragraph lengths,
  generic transitions (Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally), wrap-up sentences that restate the
  section, and promotional language in procedural contexts.
- Structural guidance we used: context before procedure, scannable organization, practical
  specificity (real names, amounts, dates, addresses), and closing with a named contact.

**no-ai-trace.md** is a full diagnostic across eight marker domains: tone and stance, vocabulary,
syntax, paragraph shape, formatting, punctuation, communication residue, and citations. What it
caught that a first read missed:

- **C1, negative parallelism** ("not X, but Y"), which it calls the single most recognizable AI
  sentence shape, and says to cut even when it feels like good writing.
- **E3, inline bold headers** followed by a colon or period. The draft's organizing skeleton was
  built from these.
- **E1** title-case headings, **E2** excessive boldface, **G5** meta-commentary on the document
  itself, **A1** significance inflation, **E5** unnecessary small tables.
- The rewrite protocol's central instruction: rebuild from the claims, not the sentences.
  Sentence-level substitution preserves AI rhythm.

## Where they disagree

Justin's guide wants second-person address, contractions, and a closing offer of help with a
named contact. The Action Plan is addressed to AAC&U reviewers, so it keeps a formal register
and the collective "we." The no-ai-trace guide is explicit that this is correct: de-AI'ing is
not de-formalizing, and formal writing can be marker-free.

## Revision results on the Action Plan

All negative parallelism removed (26 instances), all em dashes removed (39), plus significance
inflation, "by construction," "tends to," stray intensifiers, meta-commentary, and six
non-template inline bold headers. Template-derived bold headers were kept. Word count went from
3,454 to 3,264.

One unmet standard: Justin's practical-specificity rule. The plan still says "whoever controls
professional development funds" where his guide would want the name and the amount.
