# Candidate Goals — Cross-Discipline Team at a Community College

A menu to argue with, not a plan. Pick 3–5 for the Action Plan; the template asks for goals in
four types, so aim for coverage across them rather than five goals of the same kind.

**Legend**
`[C]` content goal (the *what*) · `[P]` process goal (the *how* and *who*) ·
`[E]` equity goal · `[O]` outcome goal (the *why*, for students and institution)
**★** = realistically achievable in fall 2026, the kick-off Action Plan's scope.

**The cross-discipline advantage.** A team spanning disciplines can do one thing a
single-department team can't: produce something that holds *across* wildly different courses —
a welding lab, an English 101 section, and a nursing clinical. Goals that only make sense in
one discipline waste the team's composition. Goals below are chosen to exploit the spread.

**The community-college constraints that should shape every goal.** Adjunct-heavy faculty with
no course release and often no paid professional-development time. Students who commute, work,
and juggle care responsibilities. Open admissions, so an enormous range of preparation in one
room. A dual mission — transfer *and* workforce — that pulls AI questions in two directions.
Guided pathways and transfer articulation constrain what you can change in a curriculum and how
fast. Accreditation cycles and, at many colleges, a faculty bargaining agreement that governs
workload and evaluation. A goal that ignores these reads well and dies in November.

---

## 1. AI literacy across the curriculum

- ★ `[C]` Draft a **single AI literacy definition** for the college — what any graduate should
  understand about AI regardless of program — and get it in front of the curriculum committee
  before the term ends.
- ★ `[C][P]` Map where AI competencies *already* appear in existing program outcomes across at
  least four disparate programs, rather than proposing new outcomes. Cheaper politically, and
  the map is a real artifact.
- `[C]` Write **program-level AI outcomes for one transfer pathway and one professional-technical
  pathway**, deliberately paired to show the definition survives both.
- ★ `[E]` Audit whether AI literacy work reaches developmental-education, ESL, and adult-basic-ed
  students, or only college-level courses. On most campuses it's the latter by default.
- `[O]` Get AI literacy language adopted into the college's general-education outcomes or
  institutional learning outcomes — the durable version of this work.

## 2. Teaching, assessment, and course design

- ★ `[C][P]` Produce a **cross-disciplinary assignment redesign kit**: 6–10 real assignments,
  one per discipline on the team, each shown before-and-after with the reasoning written out.
  Concrete, shareable, and it's exactly the Section 3 artifact the Final Report wants.
- ★ `[C]` Build a **course-level AI use scale** (e.g. no AI / AI for brainstorming / AI as
  drafting partner / AI-integrated) with model syllabus language for each tier, so faculty
  choose a level instead of writing policy from scratch.
- `[C][P]` Pilot **process-visible assessment** — drafts, oral defenses, annotated prompt logs,
  in-class checkpoints — in a handful of courses across disciplines and compare what happened.
- ★ `[E]` Write the syllabus language and the scale in plain language at an accessible reading
  level, and translate for the college's largest non-English home-language population.
- `[O]` Reduce academic-integrity referrals tied to AI while *increasing* the number of courses
  with explicit AI expectations — the pairing is the point, and both numbers are countable.

## 3. Policy, governance, and academic integrity

- ★ `[C]` Draft **interim AI guidance** — guidance, not policy — that faculty can use this term
  while the real governance process runs. Fast, low-stakes, buys credibility.
- `[P]` Establish a **standing cross-functional AI group** with a charter, membership, and a
  reporting line into an existing governance body. Beware: a new committee with no charge is
  the most common way this work stalls.
- ★ `[P]` Get AI on the agenda of three bodies that don't currently discuss it — e.g. curriculum
  committee, student services leadership, and the professional-technical advisory boards.
- `[C]` Revise the academic integrity policy to be **behavior-based rather than tool-based**, so
  it doesn't need rewriting with each product release, and so it doesn't rest on detection
  software.
- ★ `[E]` Adopt an explicit **no-AI-detection-as-evidence** stance, or at minimum document the
  false-positive risk for multilingual and neurodivergent writers, and require corroborating
  evidence before any accusation.
- `[P]` Clarify where AI sits relative to the **bargaining agreement** — workload, intellectual
  property in course materials, use of AI in faculty evaluation. Unglamorous; prevents a
  blow-up later.

## 4. Faculty development — designed for adjuncts

- ★ `[P][E]` Build professional development that an **adjunct teaching at three colleges can
  actually attend**: asynchronous, under 90 minutes, stipended if any money exists, and offered
  at more than one time.
- ★ `[P]` Recruit and support a small cohort of **AI teaching fellows across divisions** — one
  per division, visible, with something specific to deliver by term's end.
- `[P]` Run a **cross-disciplinary faculty learning community** where the deliverable is a
  redesigned assignment, not attendance.
- ★ `[C]` Create a **one-page "start here" resource** for faculty who have done nothing with AI
  yet and feel behind. Low ambition, high uptake.
- `[O]` Move from a handful of early adopters to a documented share of faculty who have made a
  deliberate AI decision about their course — the honest measure, since "adoption" isn't the
  goal and "use" isn't either.

## 5. Access, equity, and the tool question

- ★ `[E]` Document the **paid-vs-free gap**: what students on free tiers get versus the paid
  versions their better-resourced peers use, and whether any course assumes paid access.
- `[E][O]` Establish that **no course may require a paid AI tool** without a funded alternative —
  the AI version of the textbook-cost fight the college has probably already had.
- ★ `[E]` Check AI tool accessibility against the college's obligations — screen readers,
  captioning, keyboard navigation — before anything gets recommended campus-wide.
- `[P]` Write **procurement and data-privacy criteria** for AI tools, covering FERPA, student
  data retention, and what happens to student work submitted to a vendor.
- `[E]` Look specifically at **veteran and military-connected students, working adults, and
  Running Start / dual-credit students** — populations a college like Olympic serves heavily and
  whose AI needs and constraints differ sharply from those of the traditional 18-year-old.

## 6. Students: competencies and workforce readiness

- ★ `[C]` Ask the **professional-technical advisory boards and local employers** what AI skills
  they now expect of graduates. One meeting, and it produces evidence nobody on campus can wave
  away.
- `[C][O]` Add a documented AI competency to **two workforce programs** where employers named a
  specific need.
- ★ `[E][P]` Create **student-facing guidance** — what AI use is and isn't allowed, how to
  disclose it, why it matters — because students are currently getting contradictory rules from
  every instructor and mostly guessing.
- `[P]` Work the **transfer question**: what do the receiving four-years expect, and does our AI
  guidance create friction in articulation agreements?
- `[O]` Students can articulate when AI helped and when it short-circuited their learning —
  measurable through reflections, and closer to the actual educational goal than any policy.

## 7. How this team works — process goals worth naming

The Action Plan explicitly asks *how do your team members and the offices/departments they
represent work together?* These are the goals that answer it.

- ★ `[P]` Commit to **one collaborative fall project** that no single office could do alone, with
  a named owner per office. The kick-off asks for exactly this.
- ★ `[P]` Each team member runs **one conversation in their own division** and reports back — the
  cheapest way to turn a small team into campus-wide reach.
- `[P]` Pair the team with **the offices that own the levers**: institutional research for data,
  the teaching-and-learning center for delivery, IT for tooling, student services for the
  student-facing side, and the faculty senate for legitimacy.
- ★ `[P]` Decide the **communication sequence** — who hears about this first, second, third — and
  write it down. First contact with the wrong group sets the work back a term.
- `[P]` Build a **peer relationship with Highline and Skagit Valley**, the other Washington
  community colleges in this cohort, and compare approaches at the mid-institute event.

## 8. Evidence and assessment

- ★ `[P]` Establish a **baseline this fall** — faculty survey, student survey, or both — so the
  spring Final Report has a before to compare against. If the team does nothing else in
  September, do this.
- `[P]` Agree with **institutional research** on two or three metrics that will actually be
  tracked, and confirm someone will pull them.
- `[C][O]` Gather a **small set of student voices** — focus group or structured reflections — on
  how AI is affecting their learning. Most persuasive material available for a faculty audience.
- `[O]` Turn one strand of this work into **scholarship or a conference presentation**, which at
  a teaching institution is often the only reward structure available for this labor.

## Choosing: a filter

Run each candidate through these before it goes in the plan.

1. **Could this team, with the offices it represents, actually do it?** If it needs a vice
   president who isn't in the room, it's a request, not a goal.
2. **Is there a fall-semester version?** The kick-off plan wants fall actions. A two-year goal
   needs a first slice that finishes by December.
3. **Does it produce an artifact?** The Final Report asks for syllabi, policies, materials,
   resources. Goals that generate a document beat goals that generate awareness.
4. **Will we know if it worked?** Section 1 of the Final Report asks for the outcome. Write the
   measure now, while it's cheap.
5. **Does it need the cross-discipline composition?** If one department could have done it
   alone, this team is the wrong vehicle.
6. **What does it cost a faculty member in time?** The honest answer determines uptake more
   than the quality of the idea.
