The two flashcard files

When you finished the reading practice, the page offered your flashcards in two formats. They have the same cards in them — use either one, or use both. Each card has the word, a short definition, and the sentence from the reading where the word appeared.

  • CSV file (a small text file ending in .csv) — for importing your flashcards into a study app like Quizlet.
  • Printable sheet (an .html file) — for printing on paper, so you can study from physical cards or cut them out.

Importing the CSV into Quizlet

Quizlet menus change from time to time, so these steps are general. If anything looks different on your screen, search Quizlet's own help for the latest instructions.

  1. Go to quizlet.com and sign in. (If you don't already have an account, sign up — Quizlet has a free tier.)
  2. Choose the option to create a new study set (sometimes shown as a "+" button or "Create").
  3. Look for an Import option on the new-set page. It's usually labeled something like "Import from Word, Excel, Google Docs."
  4. Open your CSV file in a plain text editor or spreadsheet, copy the contents, and paste them into Quizlet's import box. Set the separators to comma between term and definition and new line between cards.
  5. Click Import (or "Preview," then save). Give your set a name — the reading title is a good choice — and save it.

If Quizlet's importer asks about quotes or escaping, leave those at their defaults — the CSV the page produces uses standard quoting.

For the exact current steps, see Quizlet's help pages.

Using the printable sheet

  1. Open the .html file in any web browser.
  2. Print the page (Ctrl/Cmd + P). Plain white paper is fine.
  3. Cut along the borders of each card.
  4. To study, look at the word, try to say or write the definition aloud, then turn the card over (or check the definition side) to see if you got it.

Tip: you can also "print to PDF" from the print dialog if you'd rather save it as a file.

A few tips for studying with flashcards

You don't need a special trick — just a few small habits go a long way:

  • Short and often beats long and rare. Five or ten minutes a day for several days will stick better than one long cram session.
  • Quiz yourself before flipping the card. Say or write the definition first, then check. The effort of recalling is what builds the memory.
  • Use the example sentence. Reading the word in real context — the way it actually showed up in your reading — helps you remember how it's used, not just what it means.
  • Come back to your hard cards. Set aside the ones you missed and review just those a day or two later. You'll be surprised how quickly they get easier.

You don't need to memorize every word perfectly. The goal is to recognize them when you see them again in your reading.
